8. The five final features
   1. impressions_90d — Total GSC search impressions in the 90-day window ending at the decision moment. Available at decision time because it summarizes historical search exposure from the prior quarter.
   2. clicks_90d — Total GSC clicks in the 90-day window ending at the decision moment. Available at decision time because it summarizes historical click volume from the prior quarter.
   3. ctr — Click-through rate = clicks_90d / impressions_90d × 100, stored as percent (e.g. 0.76 = 0.76%). Available at decision time because it is computed from impression and click counts that precede the prediction moment.
   4. avg_position — Mean GSC average position over the 90-day window. Lower is better. 0 means no position data. Available at decision time because position is a historical signal from prior weeks.
   5. content_age_days — Days since content was created. Available at decision time because content registration date is known before any refresh decision.

9. Why each feature is available at decision time
   - impressions_90d: Summarizes historical search exposure from the prior quarter; known before the refresh decision moment
   - clicks_90d: Summarizes historical click volume from the prior quarter; known before the refresh decision moment
   - ctr: Computed from impression and click counts that precede the prediction moment; the stored value (e.g. 0.76) is a rate from the past window
   - avg_position: Mean GSC position from prior weeks; a historical signal available before any decision
   - content_age_days: Content registration date is known before any refresh decision; age is a forward-computable quantity from content creation time

13. Main limitation
    The proxy label is not a direct ground-truth refresh label. is_declining_label = trend_direction == "down" is a provisional proxy for refresh opportunity, not a guarantee that a refresh will pay off. A declining trend may reflect: seasonality, competition (related pages absorbing demand), demand changes (shifting user interests), temporary fluctuations (short-term ranking volatility), or other external factors (algorithm updates, SERP changes). Therefore, a page labeled as "declining" may not actually benefit from a refresh intervention, and an un-declining page may still benefit from one. Refresh outcome can only be determined by running an explicit experiment or causal design.
